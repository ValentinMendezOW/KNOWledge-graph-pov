# Generative AI for the Public Sector: The Journey to Scale

**Published:** March 26, 2024
**Source:** Boston Consulting Group
**URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/gen-ai-journey-to-scale-in-government

---

## Key Takeaways

Generative AI represents a transformative opportunity for government operations. According to BCG's analysis, "potential productivity improvements from GenAI could be worth $1.75 trillion per year globally" by 2033 across all government levels. Success requires moving beyond initial pilots toward enterprise-scale implementation through six critical organizational enablers.

---

## Main Findings

### The Implementation Journey

BCG outlines a two-phase approach to GenAI adoption:

1. **Experiment and Learn** – Small teams develop proofs of concept using pilot projects with minimal risk exposure
2. **Refine, Scale, and Optimize** – Graduated expansion to enterprise-level robustness and organizational integration

### Six Critical Enablers

#### 1. Leadership
Leaders must champion GenAI adoption while addressing workforce concerns. The article notes a significant sentiment gap: "Leaders are likely to be more optimistic than employees (62% vs. 42%)" regarding GenAI benefits.

Regular users of the technology show markedly different attitudes—only 22% express concern compared to 42% among non-users, suggesting familiarity reduces apprehension.

Behavioral nudges can accelerate adoption, including making tools accessible, celebrating applications internally, and showcasing demonstrations and best practices.

#### 2. People and Skills
Cross-functional squads combining business owners, machine learning engineers, data scientists, IT experts, and governance advisors should lead initial pilots.

A significant skills gap exists: "86% of frontline employees believe they will need training," yet only 14% have received formal instruction on adapting to AI-enhanced roles.

Role evolution is inevitable. The US Department of Defense example illustrates this: their GenAI procurement tool reduces document preparation from days to 15 minutes, freeing staff for relationship-building and strategic analysis.

#### 3. Partnerships
Governments struggle attracting technology talent internally. Strategic partnerships with technology firms, universities, and industry experts accelerate capability development. The Canadian government's collaboration with Université de Montréal on AI strategies in drug discovery and health logistics exemplifies this approach.

The Australian government's Microsoft 365 Copilot trial involved 7,400 public servants, demonstrating how partnerships enable rapid scaling.

#### 4. Technology
The GenAI technology stack includes user interfaces, foundational models, datasets, and computing infrastructure. Early adoption benefits from off-the-shelf solutions, minimizing implementation barriers.

Platform selection should consider:
- Output types (text, visuals, video, code)
- Fine-tuning requirements
- Data sensitivity compliance
- Model control preferences
- Vendor security capabilities

Most governments should prioritize cloud-based deployments for flexibility and cost-effectiveness, reserving on-premises solutions for highly sensitive national security applications.

Building proprietary models typically lacks economic justification except in non-English markets or defense contexts where sovereign capability is mandatory.

#### 5. Data
GenAI performance depends on data quality and volume. The technology handles unstructured, multimodal data (text, graphics, audio, video) more effectively than traditional AI approaches, but input quality directly determines output quality.

Scaling requires modernizing legacy systems and creating unified data platforms. Data governance becomes increasingly critical when managing multimodal inputs across organizations. Governments must establish clear data provenance tracking and appropriate classification systems to prevent unauthorized usage extending beyond original purposes.

## Real-World Examples

- **Yokosuka City, Japan:** Staff trial GenAI for meeting notes, material summarization, and error-checking in low-risk applications
- **Singapore:** AI Trailblazers program directly involves public sector employees in developing new GenAI tools
- **South Australian Department of Education:** EdChat implementation using existing Microsoft Azure infrastructure with OpenAI GPT4.0
- **US Department of Defense:** Procurement documentation tool reduces creation time from days to 15 minutes

---

## Critical Considerations

Implementation requires balancing innovation with caution. Public sector organizations must maintain focus on governance, risk management, and security throughout adoption. Each government's journey should align with unique operational needs, existing technology maturity levels, and specific use case requirements.

The article emphasizes urgency: while careful implementation remains essential, delaying GenAI adoption carries significant opportunity costs as productivity and service delivery improvements accumulate.
