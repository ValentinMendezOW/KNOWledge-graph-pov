# Generative AI Holds Huge Economic Potential for the Global Economy

**Source:** McKinsey & Company (Finland)
**Date:** July 4, 2023
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/fi/news/generative-ai-holds-huge-economic-potential-for-the-global-economy

---

## Overview

McKinsey's research reveals that generative AI applications like ChatGPT and Midjourney have the potential to deliver substantial economic benefits valued as much as **$2.6 to $4.4 trillion annually** to the global economy. This article, published by McKinsey Finland, summarizes the key economic findings from McKinsey's broader research on generative AI's productivity frontier.

## Key Findings

### Economic Value

- Generative AI could add **$2.6 trillion to $4.4 trillion annually** to the global economy across 63 analyzed use cases.
- This is roughly equivalent to adding an entire economy the size of the **United Kingdom** ($3.1 trillion GDP in 2021).
- When accounting for broader integration into existing software systems, the total economic impact could reach up to **$7.9 trillion annually**.
- This represents a **15 to 40 percent increase** to the overall impact of all artificial intelligence.

### Productivity Impact

- Generative AI has the potential to increase **global labor productivity by 0.1 to 0.6 percent annually** through 2040.
- Finland specifically could experience slightly higher productivity increases of roughly **0.2 to 0.7 percent annually**.
- Compared to earlier forms of AI (machine learning and deep learning), generative AI could increase productivity by **up to 40 percent**.
- Combined with all automation technologies, productivity growth could reach **0.2 to 3.3 percentage points annually**.

### Workforce Automation

- Generative AI can automate tasks that absorb **60 to 70 percent of employees' time** today, freeing up time from routine work for creative work and innovation.
- Previous estimates had suggested only 50% of work time could be automated.
- Half of today's work activities could be automated between **2030 and 2060**, with a midpoint in **2045** — roughly a decade earlier than previous estimates.

### Impact on Knowledge Workers

- Generative AI is expected to have the **greatest impact on higher-wage and highly educated knowledge workers**.
- Greater estimated impact corresponds to higher levels of education.
- Unlike previous waves of automation technology that mostly affected physical work activities, generative AI primarily impacts knowledge work — particularly decision-making and collaborative activities.
- **40% of work activities** require median-level natural language understanding.
- **25% of total work time** involves natural language processing tasks.

### Value Distribution by Business Function

Approximately **75% of the value** from generative AI use cases falls across four areas:

1. **Customer operations** — Productivity gains valued at 30-45% of current function costs
2. **Marketing and sales** — Marketing productivity increases valued at 5-15% of global spending; sales productivity gains of 3-5%
3. **Software engineering** — Reduced code generation time and improved developer satisfaction
4. **Research and development** — Foundation models completing lead identification in weeks rather than months

### Industry-Specific Impact

- **Banking:** $200 billion to $340 billion annually (2.8-4.7% of annual revenues)
- **Retail and Consumer Packaged Goods:** $400 billion to $660 billion annually (1.2-2.0% of annual revenues)
- **High Tech:** Among the industries with the biggest percentage-of-revenue impact
- **Life Sciences:** 2.6-4.5% of annual revenues, particularly significant given ~20% R&D spending ratios

## Research Methodology

- McKinsey analyzed **63 use cases across 16 business functions**
- The study examined **850 occupations and 2,100 detailed work activities**
- Coverage spanned **47 countries**, representing more than **80% of the global workforce**

## Challenges and Implementation

- A full realization of the technology's benefits will take time
- Leaders in business and society face considerable challenges including:
  - Managing the risks inherent in generative AI
  - Determining what new skills the workforce will need
  - Rethinking core business processes for reskilling programs
- Only **1% of business leaders** describe generative AI rollouts as "mature"
- **92% of organizations** plan increased AI investments over three years
