# GenAI Promised a Creative Revolution. Can Marketers Deliver?

**Published:** October 01, 2025
**Source:** Boston Consulting Group
**Authors:** Silvio Palumbo, Mark Abraham, Mitch Colgan, Shane Fisher, Rachael Aiken, Florian Kogler, Filipa Ivanova, Niharika Mukerji

---

## Overview

Despite widespread optimism about generative AI's creative potential, enterprise deployment remains limited. A BCG survey of 251 global marketing leaders reveals a significant gap between enthusiasm and implementation, with only one-third regularly using GenAI tools while two-thirds employ them selectively or not at all.

## Key Findings

### The Optimism-Action Gap

- **92% of surveyed marketers** express optimism or strong optimism regarding GenAI's creative possibilities over the next 2-3 years
- Yet **only 33% report** end-to-end or regular GenAI usage
- **66% use the technology** selectively or avoid it entirely

### Preferred Use Cases

Marketers predominantly favor structured, templated applications:
- Copywriting and editing
- Social media content
- Internal communications

Fewer than 5% rate more sophisticated uses highly, including video generation, animation, TV creative, or product visualization.

### Quality Dependency on User Input

BCG's testing revealed that GenAI output quality depends significantly on prompt quality and user expertise. "Well-structured prompts and clearly defined goals" yielded strong results, while vague requests produced generic or poor-quality content.

Notably, only 16% of surveyed marketers identify human input factors as critical concerns, despite the direct correlation between prompt quality and output excellence.

### Primary Barriers to Adoption

Top concerns about GenAI performance include:
- Image realism and detail
- Brand alignment
- Originality and differentiation
- Writing quality
- Limited team proficiency in prompting skills (cited by one-third of respondents)

---

## Three Strategic Recommendations

### 1. Upskilling Employees

Organizations should continuously train teams in prompt engineering and tool utilization. This mental shift—from viewing GenAI as an "output generator" to leveraging it as a "creative co-pilot"—proves essential for quality outcomes and user adoption.

### 2. Reinventing Workflows

Rather than integrating GenAI into existing processes, leading organizations redesign workflows specifically for the technology. BCG's work with an education company demonstrated this approach, achieving "30% to 40% reduction in FTE hours for content production while maintaining internal quality standards."

Most respondents (44%) prioritize seamless workflow integration, but only 19% pursue end-to-end process transformation—a missed opportunity for significant efficiency gains.

### 3. Adopting Best-of-Breed Tools

Most surveyed companies rely on one or two general-purpose models. However, specialized solutions for specific marketing functions—from research and ideation to execution—are rapidly emerging. Deploying targeted tools across the workflow allows teams to concentrate on higher-value, strategic work.

---

## Technical Insights

BCG's comparative analysis of leading GenAI platforms found that:
- **Tool capabilities have converged significantly**, narrowing performance gaps between platforms
- Output quality under optimal conditions is "surprisingly strong"
- Brief, vague prompts consistently yield subpar or generic results
- Prompting skills, iterative refinement, and tool familiarity substantially impact results

---

## Workflow Integration Challenges

While 44% of marketers cite easier workflow integration as an adoption accelerator, GenAI's true transformative potential emerges through comprehensive process redesign rather than incremental augmentation. The distinction is critical: augmentation preserves existing structures, while transformation fundamentally reimagines how marketing teams operate.

---

## Looking Forward

Tool performance and public sentiment continue improving, positioning the next adoption phase around operational readiness. Success requires simultaneous focus on talent development, process innovation, and strategic tool selection—moving beyond experimentation toward scaled implementation that delivers measurable business value.
