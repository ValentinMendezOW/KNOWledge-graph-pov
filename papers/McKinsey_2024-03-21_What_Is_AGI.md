## Overview

Although generative AI tools such as ChatGPT may seem like a great leap forward, in reality they are just a step in the direction of an even greater breakthrough: artificial general intelligence, or AGI. This McKinsey Explainer explores what AGI is, how it differs from today's narrow AI, the eight capabilities AI must master to reach AGI, and what the future may hold.

## What Is AGI?

Artificial general intelligence (AGI) is a theoretical AI system with capabilities that rival those of a human. More specifically, AGI could someday replicate human-like cognitive abilities including:

- **Reasoning**
- **Problem solving**
- **Perception**
- **Learning**
- **Language comprehension**

By contrast, today's latest generative AI technologies — including ChatGPT, DALL-E, and others — are essentially **prediction machines**, albeit very good ones. They can predict, with a high degree of accuracy, the answer to a specific prompt because they have been trained on huge amounts of data. AGI tools, however, could feature cognitive and emotional abilities (like empathy) indistinguishable from those of a human.

## AGI vs. Narrow AI

**Narrow AI** (also called artificial narrow intelligence, or ANI) is the application of AI techniques to a specific and well-defined problem. Examples include:

- Chatbots like ChatGPT
- Algorithms that spot fraud in credit card transactions
- Natural-language-processing engines that quickly process thousands of legal documents
- Self-driving car systems
- Image recognition tools

Unlike narrow AI, whose competence is confined to well-defined tasks, an AGI system would be able to **generalize knowledge, transfer skills between domains, and solve novel problems** without task-specific reprogramming.

Some machine-learning algorithms are specialized in training themselves to detect patterns — this is called **deep learning**. Deep learning has enabled major advances, but it still falls far short of human-level general intelligence.

## The Eight Capabilities AI Needs to Master Before Achieving AGI

McKinsey identifies eight key capabilities that AI must achieve at a human level before AGI becomes a reality. Notably, estimates for when these capabilities will be reached have shifted significantly compared to pre-generative-AI predictions from 2017, with most now expected sooner:

### 1. Sensory Perception
AI systems are far away from developing human-like sensory-perception capabilities. For example, systems trained through deep learning still have **poor color consistency**: self-driving car systems have been fooled by small pieces of black tape or stickers on a red stop sign. For any human, the redness of the stop sign is still completely evident, but the deep-learning-based system gets fooled into thinking the stop sign is something else.

### 2. Natural-Language Understanding
AI will need to be able to consume sources of information — books, articles, blog posts — with full comprehension. Humans write with implicit assumptions about the reader's general knowledge, and true understanding requires grasping context, nuance, and cultural background.
**Estimated human-level performance:** ~2025

### 3. Natural-Language Generation
The ability to produce coherent, contextually appropriate language output. Large language models (LLMs) have made significant strides here, but true AGI-level generation would require genuine understanding, not just pattern matching.

### 4. Logical Reasoning and Problem Solving
The capacity to apply logic, draw inferences, and solve novel problems across domains.
**Estimated human-level performance:** ~2023

### 5. Creativity
Generating novel patterns, ideas, and categories. The emergence of generative AI has accelerated progress in this area significantly.
**Estimated human-level performance:** ~2023
**Pre-GenAI estimate (2017):** Much later

### 6. Social and Emotional Capabilities
This encompasses three sub-dimensions:
- **Social and emotional sensing** — detecting and interpreting human emotions
  **Estimated:** ~2030
- **Social and emotional output** — producing appropriate emotional responses
  **Estimated:** ~2031
- **Social and emotional reasoning** — understanding why people feel and act the way they do
  **Estimated:** ~2033

### 7. Fine Motor Skills
Fine motor skills are critical. Any human can easily retrieve a set of keys from a pocket, yet very few would let current robot manipulators or humanoid hands perform that task. Physical dexterity and manipulation in unstructured environments remain extremely challenging.

### 8. Coordination with Multiple Agents
The ability to work alongside other AI systems and humans in complex, dynamic environments.
**Estimated human-level performance:** ~2035

## How Far Away Is AGI?

- **Most researchers and academics believe we are decades away** from realizing AGI.
- **Some predict we will not see AGI this century — or ever.**
- McKinsey contends that achieving AGI remains a **distant prospect**, positioning it as a theoretical technology that would require decades or centuries to develop.
- Despite the uncertainty, McKinsey advises that **executives should begin preparing now** for this potential future development.

## AGI vs. Generative AI

| Dimension | Generative AI | AGI |
|-----------|--------------|-----|
| **Scope** | Specific tasks (text, image, code generation) | All cognitive tasks a human can perform |
| **Understanding** | Pattern matching on training data | True comprehension and reasoning |
| **Emotional intelligence** | Simulated responses | Genuine empathy and emotional understanding |
| **Adaptability** | Limited to training domain | Can transfer knowledge across any domain |
| **Current status** | Deployed and scaling | Purely theoretical |

## Key Takeaways

1. **AGI is theoretical** — no AGI system exists today, and it may be decades or more away.
2. **Current AI is narrow** — even the most impressive generative AI tools are prediction machines, not general intelligences.
3. **Eight capabilities** must reach human-level performance before AGI is possible, spanning perception, language, reasoning, creativity, emotional intelligence, motor skills, and multi-agent coordination.
4. **Timelines have accelerated** — the emergence of generative AI in 2022-2023 moved many capability estimates forward compared to 2017 projections.
5. **Business leaders should prepare** — despite AGI's distance, understanding its trajectory is important for long-term strategic planning.

## Implications for Business

McKinsey emphasizes that even though AGI remains far off, the journey toward it is already producing transformative narrow AI tools. Organizations should:

- Invest in understanding and deploying current AI capabilities
- Monitor developments in the eight key capability areas
- Build organizational AI literacy
- Consider long-term scenarios that include more capable AI systems
- Focus on human-AI collaboration frameworks
