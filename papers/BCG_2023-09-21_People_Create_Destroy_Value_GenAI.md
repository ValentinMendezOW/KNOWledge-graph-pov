# How People Can Create—and Destroy—Value with Generative AI

**Published:** September 21, 2023
**Source:** Boston Consulting Group
**Authors:** François Candelon, Lisa Krayer, Saran Rajendran, David Zuluaga Martínez

## Overview

BCG conducted a landmark scientific study with 758 consultants to examine how generative AI (specifically GPT-4) affects professional performance. The research reveals a paradoxical finding: people tend to trust GenAI where it struggles and distrust it where it excels.

## Key Findings

**Performance Impact Varies by Task Type:**

- **Creative Innovation:** ~90% of participants improved performance when using GPT-4, achieving results 40% higher than controls. Participants performed best when they avoided editing the AI's output.

- **Business Problem-Solving:** Users of GPT-4 performed 23% worse than those working without the tool on complex analytical tasks requiring nuanced judgment.

**The Trust Paradox:**

Participants demonstrated misaligned confidence levels. While they doubted GenAI's creative capabilities (where it succeeds), they accepted its analytical recommendations uncritically, even when warned about potential errors. The tool's persuasive output made flawed reasoning appear credible.

**Group-Level Consequences:**

GenAI reduced ideation diversity by 41%, with participants generating similar ideas despite individual performance gains. Semantic overlap between AI-assisted and human-only outputs remained minimal (<10%), suggesting standardization of thinking patterns.

**Performance Distribution:**

GenAI compressed performance variance dramatically. Lower-baseline performers nearly matched higher-baseline peers when using the technology, reducing differentiation among workers.

## Training Limitations

A 30-minute training session on GenAI best practices and limitations did not prevent misuse on analytical tasks. Counterintuitively, trained participants performed worse than untrained users, suggesting overconfidence effects may undermine safety protocols.

## Strategic Implications

**Organizational Challenges:**

1. **Data Infrastructure:** Competitive advantage requires fine-tuning GenAI with proprietary, high-quality data—most organizations lack sufficient engineering capabilities.

2. **Workflow Redesign:** Leaders must shift mindsets from "AI generates drafts requiring human refinement" to "AI produces near-final outputs requiring verification against guardrails."

3. **Workforce Planning:** Organizations face four critical unknowns: identifying necessary human capabilities, designing hiring strategies when GenAI levels proficiency differences, developing effective training beyond compliance, and preserving cognitive diversity.

4. **Experimentation:** Given rapid GenAI evolution, continuous testing through dedicated innovation labs remains essential.

**Human Value Creation:**

Participants intuitively recognized that meaningful work centers on tasks beyond GenAI's frontier—complex change management, strategic adaptation, and context-specific application. Employees expressed optimism about transitioning from routine work to higher-value activities.

## Emerging Concerns

**Creativity Atrophy:**

Roughly 70% of participants worried that extensive GenAI reliance might erode their creative abilities over time, comparing it to GPS dependency diminishing navigation skills.

**Long-term Innovation Risk:**

Collective ideation homogenization could reduce organizational innovation capacity, creating competitive vulnerability as firms adopt similar GenAI approaches across comparable task sets.

## Experimental Methodology

The rigorous study design included:
- Baseline proficiency measurement
- Random assignment to treatment groups
- Blind grading by humans and independent GPT-4 evaluation
- Incentive alignment with performance reviews and bonuses
- Control for 20+ demographic and behavioral variables

## Conclusion

GenAI adoption represents a fundamental change management imperative requiring strategic data capabilities, workflow transformation, and continuous workforce evolution. Success depends on organizations learning and adapting faster than ever before, with leaders making critical decisions about which tasks genuinely benefit from AI assistance versus those requiring human judgment.
