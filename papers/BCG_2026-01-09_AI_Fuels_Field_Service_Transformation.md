## Key Takeaways

- AI can reshape field service operations when coupled with robust change management and employee engagement
- Successful implementations allocate 70% of effort to people and processes, not just technology
- Phased approaches (design, pilot, scale) with continuous feedback drive adoption and sustained performance improvements

## Main Content

### The AI Opportunity in Field Service

Artificial intelligence offers significant potential for field service operations—the frontline work maintaining and repairing critical assets like factory equipment and transportation fleets. BCG's Field Service AI product has delivered tangible results, producing productivity gains between 10-15% and margin expansion of 5-10%.

However, technology alone doesn't guarantee success. Organizations realizing these benefits combine cutting-edge tools with structured change management. The critical insight: new technology must be both usable and actively used by frontline teams.

### The 10-20-70 Rule

BCG emphasizes a specific allocation formula for AI transformation efforts:

- **10%** dedicated to specialized machine learning models
- **20%** devoted to data infrastructure and technological backbone
- **70%** focused on people, processes, training, and cultural transformation

This distribution reflects that technology implementation succeeds only when paired with organizational readiness and employee capability.

### Five Core Principles for Successful Change

**1. Activate Leadership**

Leaders must serve as genuine change agents rather than passive endorsers. Effective approaches include:

- Clear messaging demonstrating how AI empowers rather than displaces workers
- Consistent metric cascades breaking down organizational KPIs into actionable targets across management levels
- Alignment of leadership, management, and frontline staff around shared goals

**2. Engage Service Teams**

Frontline involvement drives better outcomes. Best practices include:

- Co-developing solutions with technicians to ensure tools address real-world challenges
- Continuous feedback collection from design through scaling phases
- Responsive refinement as needs evolve

**3. Foster Executional Excellence**

Synchronized implementation requires:

- Alignment of service roles and organizational structures with AI-enabled models
- Clear ownership and accountability for initiatives
- Data-enabled operating rhythms (daily standups, weekly reviews)
- Emphasis on responsible AI practices ensuring transparency and fairness

**4. Create High-Performance Culture**

AI enables workforce transitions toward higher-value activities. Organizations should:

- Shift operations managers from manual scheduling to strategic decision-making
- Increase technician "wrench time" by reducing documentation burdens
- Redirect back-office staff from administrative tasks to customer relationships
- Foster collaboration through modern communication tools and gamification
- Share success stories and celebrate peer achievements

**5. Enable and Empower**

Supporting employee adaptation requires:

- Accelerated, hands-on training programs
- Designated AI champions within service teams supporting peer transitions
- Train-the-trainer initiatives building sustainable learning ecosystems

## Implementation Roadmap: Three Phases

### Design Phase

This initial stage emphasizes collaborative solution development:

- Bring together leadership and frontline workers to ensure designs meet operational needs
- Identify and engage champion technicians, operations managers, and support staff
- Build ownership among future users through early participation
- Develop comprehensive deployment roadmaps and communication strategies

**Example:** A food-processing machinery manufacturer engaged end customers weekly during design, uncovering pain points and testing prototypes. This collaborative approach produced solutions reflecting user realities, strengthening adoption and generating early wins worth more than $100 million in recurring revenue potential.

### Pilot Phase

Structured pilots test solutions in real-world conditions:

- Select specific locations for controlled implementation
- Activate champion networks to support peers and provide feedback
- Create accessible feedback loops encouraging input from all users
- Continuously evaluate adoption rates and engagement levels
- Implement practical suggestions collaboratively

Low adoption signals warrant re-engagement to identify gaps and explore enhancements.

**Example:** A heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning equipment manufacturer used phased rollouts with train-the-trainer programs, expanding location by location. Cross-functional collaboration among technicians, service managers, parts managers, and regional leadership built trust in the solution. Post-implementation, annual revenue growth accelerated from 5% to over 15%, with EBIT doubling within 12 months.

### Scale and Sustain Phase

Expansion builds on pilot successes:

- Integrate lessons learned into broader rollouts
- Employ demonstrations and success stories as pull-forward mechanisms
- Leverage champions as coaches and mentors
- Schedule regular roadmap discussions including frontline teams
- Implement ongoing training combining onsite and virtual sessions
- Establish continuous improvement processes

**Example:** A major trucking company strengthened collaboration between sales and customer success teams after customers appreciated AI insights but wanted guidance connecting findings to actionable improvements and measurable returns. After upskilling team members and developing ROI quantification playbooks, customer conversion rates increased over 40%, improving retention and expanding wallet share.

## Outcomes of Effective Implementation

When organizations execute these principles systematically, measurable shifts occur:

- **Operations managers** transition from administrative scheduling to strategic optimization
- **Field technicians** gain increased technical work time (wrench time) by reducing documentation and manual research
- **Back-office staff** reduce administrative burden and increase customer-facing activities
- **Overall business metrics** show improved productivity, margin expansion, and revenue growth

---

## Conclusion

Field service transformation through AI succeeds when technology implementation receives approximately equal attention to organizational change. The most successful organizations recognize that tools without engaged, trained, and empowered people deliver limited value. By following structured phases emphasizing collaboration, feedback, and continuous learning, companies unlock AI's potential while building sustainable competitive advantage.
