# Generative AI in M&A: You're Not Behind—Yet

**Published:** February 4, 2025
**Authors:** Jeff Haxer, Maja Omanovic, Ben Siegal, Brooke Houston (Bain & Company)

---

## Executive Summary

This report examines how generative AI is reshaping mergers and acquisitions, revealing that while adoption remains nascent, early movers are gaining competitive advantages. Companies that delay implementation risk falling behind in deal sourcing, valuation, and execution.

---

## Key Findings

**Current Adoption Rates:**
- Only 21% of surveyed M&A practitioners currently use generative AI (up from 16% in 2023)
- 36% of the most active acquirers employ the technology
- Over 60% of private equity firms use generative AI tools
- More than half of all companies expect deployment by 2027

**Performance Impact:**

The report emphasizes that "companies mastering generative AI in M&A over the next five years will identify targets faster, underwrite deals with greater confidence, execute diligence more rapidly, and ultimately deliver stronger returns."

---

## Three Competitive Advantages for Early Adopters

**1. Superior Deal Assessment**

Early adopters reduce data summarization from one week to approximately one day, enabling teams to focus on value extraction analysis rather than administrative compilation. This efficiency allows companies to distinguish promising opportunities from problematic deals quickly.

**2. Enhanced Value Recognition**

Advanced tools analyze cross-selling potential, synergy opportunities, and revenue integration by processing sales data, customer information, and catalog details. This capability enables more accurate pre-close underwriting and faster post-acquisition value capture.

**3. Reduced Employee Burden**

Approximately 80% of companies using generative AI report decreased manual workload, allowing business leaders to balance M&A responsibilities with operational duties, thereby supporting retention and performance.

---

## Strategic Recommendations for Late Adopters

**Start Immediately:** Building expertise requires experimentation. Companies cannot replicate early movers' learning curves by purchasing solutions later.

**Build Incrementally:** Start with well-engineered prompts using quality data sources, then scale to advanced solutions.

**Innovate Purposefully:** Move beyond automation to redesign end-to-end processes leveraging AI capabilities while protecting competitive advantages.

**Evolve Talent Strategy:** Shift focus from project management tasks toward strategic value creation as AI handles administrative work.

---

## Future Timeline

Within 12 months, integration workplans and transition agreements will take less than 20% of previous timeframes. Within five years, every M&A stage will incorporate generative AI capabilities.

---

## Risk for Laggards

Companies not investing in generative AI face three vulnerabilities:

- **Bidding disadvantage:** Competitors with superior analysis will outbid them on quality opportunities
- **Synergy capture gaps:** Later identification and realization of value creation opportunities
- **Workforce challenges:** Employees stretched across M&A and operational demands face burnout and turnover

---

## Conclusion

The performance disparity between active and infrequent acquirers will widen substantially due to generative AI adoption. While 21% current usage suggests no immediate crisis, the trajectory demands urgent action for organizations seeking to remain competitive in dealmaking.
