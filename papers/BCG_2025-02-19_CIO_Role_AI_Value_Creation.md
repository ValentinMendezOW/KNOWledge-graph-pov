# The CIO's Role in AI Value Creation

**Published:** February 19, 2025
**Source:** Boston Consulting Group
**URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/cios-role-in-ai-transformation-and-productivity

## Key Takeaways

CIOs can leverage generative AI to enhance productivity within their tech functions while simultaneously driving broader organizational AI transformation. The research indicates that "GenAI can address up to 50% of tech function costs" with efficiency improvements reaching 30%. Three high-value use cases emerge as particularly promising: programming assistants, IT service desk automation, and knowledge management tools powered by natural language interfaces.

## Main Findings

### Current AI Adoption Gap

BCG's global study of 1,000 senior executives reveals a significant implementation challenge: only 26% of companies successfully create value from AI. More than half identify legacy IT architectures as barriers to scaling, reusability, and data accessibility. While adoption rates for specific GenAI use cases remain in single digits across industries, the untapped opportunity is substantial.

### Financial Impact Potential

Organizations implementing high-value GenAI applications in IT can achieve net savings of approximately 10% in tech function expenses. The calculation accounts for addressable costs reductions (up to 50%), efficiency gains (up to 30%), and GenAI-specific expenses (approximately 5% increase).

### Leadership Differentiation

A striking statistic emerges from the research: "At 86% of AI future-built companies, IT leads or co-leads with the business on GenAI," compared to 54% at stagnating companies. This demonstrates that CIO involvement directly correlates with AI success.

## CIO Strategic Priorities

### 1. Optimizing IT Productivity

**High-Value Use Cases:**
- **Programming assistants** boost software development productivity by over 30%, enabling developers to focus on strategic work
- **Service desk automation** handles routine tickets through self-service and task automation; one global insurance provider estimates GenAI can manage 75% of tickets
- **Knowledge management** leverages natural language processing for intuitive access to organizational documentation
- **Legacy modernization** accelerates system updates through automated code comprehension and documentation
- **Cybersecurity enhancement** identifies subtle threats by analyzing unstructured data
- **Project management** generates plans and progress reports using historical data

### 2. Expanding Organizational Influence

CIOs should lead on three interconnected challenges:

**Regulatory Compliance**
The global regulatory landscape is rapidly evolving. The European Union's AI Act requires risk classification, registration of high-risk systems, transparency measures, and data governance. Asian markets mandate transparency and accountability, while the US has seen 31 states pass varying legislation as of August 2024.

**Architectural Scalability**
Organizations need modular, reusable infrastructure supporting enterprise-wide deployment. This includes foundation models, GenAI platforms, unified data layers, and self-monitoring capabilities. The research describes mapping 300+ use cases to eight archetypal AI applications, enabling template-based deployment rather than building each solution individually.

**Shadow IT Mitigation**
Unauthorized tool adoption creates data privacy, security, and compliance risks. The solution involves establishing governance frameworks that encourage controlled experimentation within approved environments while capturing and scaling successful implementations.

## Implementation Roadmap

### Five Core Actions for IT Leadership

1. **Prioritize by value** – Assess both cost addressability and efficiency improvements; align initiatives with strategic business objectives
2. **Reimagine processes** – Apply zero-based thinking rather than automating inefficient legacy workflows
3. **Renegotiate vendor contracts** – Align payments with outcomes through performance-based or usage-based models
4. **Invest in change management** – Two-thirds of AI-leading companies heavily invest in workforce reskilling
5. **Build strategic talent** – Maintain critical capabilities in-house through hybrid approaches combining internal teams with selected vendors

### Critical Emerging Roles

Organizations must establish positions including chief AI officers, ethics and compliance officers, and prompt engineers. Existing roles require evolution: developers need AI framework integration skills; security specialists must address new data privacy risks; product managers require GenAI proficiency.

### Responsible AI Leadership

Leading companies continuously monitor compliance with responsible AI frameworks. CIOs should establish cross-functional governance teams incorporating legal, HR, and business representatives. Establishing standardized organizational definitions of AI ensures consistent understanding across departments.

## Organizational Impact

### The 10/20/70 Rule

Successful AI implementation distributes effort as: 10% on data science algorithms, 20% on modernized tech infrastructure, and 70% on processes, talent, and change management. The majority effort often receives insufficient attention, representing a common failure point.

### Governance Framework Benefits

Establishing clear governance enables organizations to "reap the benefits of experimentation without creating a zoo of tools." This controlled environment channels innovative efforts productively while maintaining oversight, enabling the identification and scaling of value-creating use cases.

## Broader Implications

The research demonstrates that CIOs positioned as innovation partners—rather than service providers—drive substantially better outcomes. GenAI investments are projected to grow 60% over three years, reaching 7.6% of IT budgets by 2027. However, since only a quarter of companies have moved beyond proof-of-concept stages, tremendous upside remains for organizations executing effectively.
